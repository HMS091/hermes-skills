# Financial Influencer Due Diligence Framework

## Overview

Framework for evaluating stock/investment influencers (大V/博主/股神) who claim exceptional returns. Example case: **Serenity** (X/Twitter "AI股神", claimed 46x in 5 months).

## Step 1: Verify Claimed Returns

**Questions to ask:**
- Is the claimed return independently verifiable? (Portfolio screenshots? Brokerage statements?)
- What was the starting capital? ($500 → $23K is impressive but not $500K → $23M)
- Are returns calculated from peak to peak, or realizable? (Unrealized gains ≠ cash)
- Is the time frame cherry-picked? (Pick the best 5 months out of 5 years?)
- Are losses disclosed alongside wins?

**Red flags:**
- No verifiable track record (only screenshots/cropped images)
- "I can't share my portfolio because..." (privacy/NDA/legal reasons are common excuses)
- Only winners are posted, losers are never mentioned
- Returns that would put them in the top 0.01% of professional traders

## Step 2: Analyze the Business Model

| Model | Signal | Risk |
|-------|--------|------|
| **Pure analysis** | Shares thesis + exits positions publicly | Low — genuine research |
| **Pump signal** | Recommends stock → fans buy → price rises | Medium — fans are exit liquidity |
| **Pump + dump** | Buys first, then recommends, then sells into rally | 🔴 **High — illegal in most jurisdictions** |
| **付费群/知识星球** | Charging for "exclusive picks" | Medium — incentive to create excitement |
| **Affiliate/ads** | Getting paid by companies to promote | Medium — biased recommendations |

**Key test:** Does the influencer disclose their position BEFORE or AFTER recommending? If they can't prove they held it before the public call, assume they bought after.

## Step 3: Quantitative Red Flags

| Indicator | What to check |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Consistency** | Do they have wins across different market conditions, or only in a raging bull market? |
| **Portfolio size** | Did they start with $500 and turn it into $23K, or claim $100K → $4.6M? Scale matters. |
| **Withdrawals** | Have they actually taken money out, or is it all "paper gains"? |
| **Position sizing** | Do they bet 100% on one stock (gambling), or have proper risk management? |
| **Stop losses** | Do they acknowledge risk management, or is it "buy and hold forever"? |

## Step 4: The "Follow the Money" Test

Ask: **Who benefits most from this recommendation?**

- If the influencer benefits from price going up (they own it), they're selling you their exit
- If they benefit from subscribers/views, they're selling entertainment, not investing advice
- If they benefit from affiliate commissions, they're selling product, not analysis

**The ultimate question:** "Would this person give me this advice if there was zero personal benefit to them?"

## Real Case: Serenity Analysis

**Claim:** 46x return in 5 months, "AI卡脖子" stock goddess on X/Twitter

**Findings:**
- Tracks AI supply chain chokepoints: InP substrates, optical interconnects, semiconductor materials
- Specific stock call: SIVE (Swedish semiconductor), target 3x
- AXTI was her early winner (9x from discovery) — but was she buying before or after telling followers?
- Multiple YouTube channels fact-checking her: "是真是神还是神话？" — divided opinions
- No verified brokerage statements or independent audit of returns

**Verdict:** Likely has genuine research ability on AI supply chain, but:
1. 46x return claim cannot be independently verified
2. Her recommendations benefit from her own follower base (self-fulfilling)
3. Best use case: **learn her sector analysis, don't copy her trades**
4. The real money was made by early followers of her first calls; later followers buy into already-pumped stocks

**Rule of thumb:** If you're hearing about an influencer's stock pick, **the easy money has already been made by people who heard about it before you.**
